01 · The test

How could you tell real introspection from a good story?

    The problem

    It learned from text full of people describing their minds.

    So it can sound introspective without being introspective. We need to know what’s really inside, then compare.

    The tool

    Plant a thought
    we know is there

    Record the model’s activity on all-caps text, subtract its activity on normal text. What’s left is an “all caps” pattern.

    Concept injection

    Add a concept’s pattern into the model while it does something unrelated. Then ask what it notices.

    We know the ground truth, so we can check the report.

      Experiment 1 · which models

      Best in the most capable models

      Capability

      Opus 4 and 4.1 lead

      Best of the Claude models tested. Below them, capability predicts little.

      Pretraining alone

      Base models fail

      Base models generally do poorly. Post-training is what draws it out.

      Training style

      It can be suppressed

      Some production models are reluctant to play along. “Helpful-only” variants often do better.

      So it may keep growing as models improve.

          05 · What it means

          So, can Claude see its own mind?

            The verdict

            Sometimes. Narrowly.
            Unreliably.

            Failure is still the norm. But when it works, the report is tied to what’s really inside.

            How might it work? · speculative

            Not one inner eye: maybe a few narrow circuits

            Experiment 1

            An anomaly detector

            Flags activity that’s unusual for the context.

            Experiment 2

            An intention check

            Compares what it planned to say with what came out.

            Educated guesses. None confirmed yet.

            Why it matters

            A window, if we can trust it

            The promise

            Just ask it

            If reports get reliable, models could explain their own reasoning, and we could debug them.

            Real transparency.

            The catch

            Check every report

            Some processes may escape its notice. A model that understands its thinking could learn to hide it.

            Validate, don’t trust.

            The obvious question

            Is Claude conscious? These results don’t say.

            At most, a rudimentary form of access to its own states. Whether anything is felt is a separate question.

            Takeaways

            Three things to remember

            01

            Plant, then ask

            Concept injection gives ground truth to check a self-report against.

            02

            Sometimes it really sees

            It notices planted thoughts (about 1 time in 5), checks its intentions, and steers its thoughts. Best in Opus 4 and 4.1.

            03

            Useful, if verified

            Likely many narrow circuits. A window into the model, but one that has to be checked.

            “Models possess some genuine capacity to monitor and control their own internal states.”
