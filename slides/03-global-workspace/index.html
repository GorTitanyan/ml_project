01 · The theory

How does a brain decide what you’re aware of?

    Global workspace theory

    Many specialists.
    One stage.

    The brain’s specialists work in parallel, unconsciously, each on its own.

    The global workspace

    A small shared stage. Whatever gets onto it is broadcast to every specialist at once, and that is what you are aware of.

    If a thought is in the workspace…

    Five properties of conscious access

    01

    Report

    You can put it into words.

    02

    Control

    You can call it up on purpose.

    03

    Reason

    You can think it through, step by step, in your head.

    04

    Flexibility

    Every specialist hears it, so any task can use it.

    05

    Selectivity

    Only some things get in. Breathing and grammar never do.

    → These become our five experiments on Claude.

    02 · The lens

    How do you find a workspace inside a neural network?

      The starting point

      A conscious thought can usually be put into words.

      So look for internal patterns that shape what the model could say — not what it’s saying right now.

      The Jacobian lens · “J-lens”

      A lens for
      words-in-waiting

      It translates what’s going on inside the model into words: the ones the model is being pushed toward saying, now or later, even if it never says them.

      The J-space

      Internal patterns, each tied to one word. A handful light up at any moment. Our candidate for Claude’s workspace.

      SilentIt never shows up in the text, not even in written-out reasoning.
      EmergentNobody designed it. It formed during training.

            The big picture

            Nobody designed it.
            It emerged.

            Maybe a workspace is a general solution that intelligent systems converge on.

            →

            It is built from potential outputs, so maybe the brain’s workspace is tied to regions that plan action and speech.

            →

            It works without recurrence, so maybe loops aren’t strictly necessary for conscious-access functions.

            Open questions

            What we don’t know yet

            1. 01Single words onlyThe lens can only name concepts that fit in a single token.
            2. 02Probably not the whole storyThe J-space is a good candidate for the workspace, but likely not all of it.
            3. 03No known gatekeeperWe don’t know what decides what enters the J-space. There are hints of self, emotion-like reactions and metacognition.
            Takeaways

            Three things to remember

            01

            A workspace emerged

            A few dozen word-like thoughts that Claude can report, control and reason with. Automatic skills bypass it. Nobody designed it.

            02

            We can read it, and edit it

            It reveals what the model doesn’t say: that it suspects a test, that it is fabricating, that it has a planted goal. Edits steer its answers.

            03

            Access, not experience

            It matches what conscious access does. Whether anything is felt remains open.
